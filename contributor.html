Scientists behind the plants

Behind every species on Canopy is a scientist who first collected, described or studied it. This page introduces four of them: an Italian explorer who collected in Sarawak, a Spanish friar in the Philippines, a British botanist who later led Kew, and the eighteenth-century founder of plant naming. Hover over a card, or tap it on a phone, to see what they studied and how their work shaped what we know about the plant.

How a plant gets its scientific name

Every plant on Canopy carries a two-part scientific name. The work of the scientists above follows the same basic path that botanists still use today.

  1. A botanist collects a specimen, ideally with flowers or fruit, and records where it was found.
  2. The specimen is pressed and stored in a herbarium so that others can examine it later.
  3. The botanist compares it with known species and, if it is new, publishes a formal description.
  4. The species receives its name, often honouring a place or a person, as Hopea beccariana does for Odoardo Beccari.
Herbarium
A collection of pressed, labelled plant specimens kept for study.
Type specimen
The specimen that a species name is permanently tied to.
Epithet
The second word of a scientific name, such as beccariana in Hopea beccariana.
